How frctlns finds overlaps

Places that overlap: for the day you stand on, frctlns looks three ways at once — four when you came with a word — and lists the days it found, the most overlap first.

At 00:00: where the market stood over the 7 days up to each day — price, market cap, volume, volatility and 24h / 7d / 30d change, each as a place within its own past year, and Fear & Greed on its own 0–100 scale. Only what was out by 00:00 UTC of each day is used, and an earlier day counts only if its own next 30 days had already happened. Near the start of the archive, where fewer than five earlier days qualify, later days fill in and are shown as later.

Afterwards (hindsight): the shape of the price from 2 days before to a week after. Only the shape counts — a smaller move, or one that took half or twice as long, can still match.

Together (hindsight): the set of windows that made an unusual jump on the day or the day after — a change at least 3× that window's usual day-to-day change over the year before — found again on another day. The directions are shown, not required.

The word: the days a word left public traces — its English Wikipedia article read far more than usual (from 2015-07-01), or a Hacker News story with it in the title at its most points. Found with today's records; not an event detector.

What overlapped? measures each pair all three ways, with the same conditions; the order of the places is the mean of the three.

When data is missing: only what both days have is compared, and a day is compared only if at least half of the market can be compared. A day in a coin's first year has a short past, and is marked so.

Hindsight: afterwards, together and what moved with it use what happened after the day, and say so. What came after is always shown; at 00:00 never uses it.

Fear & Greed is CMC's index for the whole crypto market, and the Bitcoin network is Bitcoin's own chain: both are the same for every coin.

frctlns observes; it does not predict. It is not investment advice.
