How frctlns finds overlaps

Places that overlap: for the day you stand on, frctlns looks in several ways at once and lists every day it found, in time order — not ranked. Each place carries a mark for the way it overlapped: at 00:00 (the market stood alike), afterwards (the price moved in the same shape), N windows (the same windows jumped together), or the word you came with. Each way uses one fixed condition, written under “Why this pair?”. What each way found nothing for is said under the list.

Where it stood (STATE) puts each axis on a 0–1 scale: where it stood within its own past year, using only data up to that day. Price, market cap, volatility and the change figures use the past-year range; volume is compared on a log scale; Fear & Greed is its 0–100 value. With less than 30 days of past an axis is absent.

How it moved (TRAJECTORY) compares how things moved inside the window, from its first day: price as % change, volume against the window's median (log), volatility as is, Fear & Greed as points gained or lost. Each axis has a fixed scale, so a match does not depend on which other windows were searched.

Strict time (at 00:00): a past day only counts if its next 30 days had already happened by D, and nothing after D is used to find it. What came after is shown next to the pair, never used to choose it.

Later in history: near the start of the archive there may be fewer than five such days. Only then, frctlns fills the list with days from after D, marked “later in history”. Their replays start after D's own next 30 days, so D's future still takes no part in finding them; what they show is hindsight.

Spacing: the look-alike days are at least 30 days apart, so one long stretch of the market is not shown five times. If several still come from the same season, that stretch really did last.

Short and incomplete history: a day in the archive's first year has less than a year behind it (short history); a later day can have gaps in its past year (incomplete history). Both stay in the list, marked with how many days were there to compare.

Only axes present on both sides are compared; their weights are shared out again. A past day is compared only if at least half of D's axes (by weight) can be compared there — the ones left out are counted, not hidden.

Afterwards (hindsight) is not where the market stood, but how it moved. It compares the shape of the price from a little before the day to a week after it, with every other day in the archive, earlier or later. Only the shape counts — a move a third of the size, or one that took half or twice as long, can still match — and each result shows how much it really moved. It uses what happened after the day, so it is marked hindsight.

Together (hindsight) takes the set of windows that made an unusual jump on your day or the day after, and looks across the whole archive for the other days when all of them jumped again — the same windows, whichever way they went; the directions are shown, not required.

What moved with it? (hindsight) lays the same 38 days — a week before the day to a month after — under each other, one line per window: the coin's price, Fear & Greed and the Bitcoin network; the coin on Wikipedia and on Hacker News; Nasdaq, VIX and the US dollar (FRED); and all of English Wikipedia. Each line is scaled to its own range. A dot marks a day that was unusual for that window: a change at least three times its usual day-to-day change over the year before (for Hacker News, at least three times the month's usual count). frctlns does not say what caused what. Some windows move together, some later, some not at all — and nothing lining up is a result too.

What overlapped? measures the pair every way, with the same conditions, whichever way found it. The windows of a pair are ordered by how alike the two days look through each one: 1 − the mean gap between their places within each one's own past year (for the market, every part of it). Headlines, pictures and the X door have no number, so they come after.

Fear & Greed is CMC's index for the whole crypto market. It is the same for every coin: on an ETH day it tells the mood of the market around ETH, not ETH's own.

Other windows (Wikipedia, weather, the Bitcoin network and the rest) never change which days are found. They only show how that day looked from somewhere else, using what had come out by 00:00 UTC of each day. Attention follows the coin you chose (its English Wikipedia article); the Bitcoin network is Bitcoin's own chain, shown for every coin.

From the world: a word, topic or event finds the days it left public traces — the day its English Wikipedia article was read far more than usual (from 2015-07-01), or the day a Hacker News story with the word in its title got its most points (from 2013). These are attention traces found with today's records, not an event detector. Through the windows, each day shows what had come out by its 00:00 UTC; the day itself is shown after that, as recorded now.

frctlns shows what followed similar days. It is not a forecast and not investment advice.
